Creamy Tuscan Chicken

Succulent pan-seared chicken breasts in a rich, creamy sauce with sun-dried tomatoes, fresh spinach, and aromatic garlic. This Italian-inspired comfort food is restaurant-quality but simple enough for a weeknight dinner.

Ingredients

  • 4 boneless, skinless chicken breasts
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 cup heavy cream
  • 1/2 cup chicken broth
  • 1/2 cup sun-dried tomatoes, chopped
  • 2 cups fresh spinach
  • 1/2 cup grated Parmesan cheese
  • 1 teaspoon Italian seasoning
  • Salt and pepper to taste

Steps

  1. Season chicken breasts with salt, pepper, and Italian seasoning on both sides.
  2. Heat olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add chicken and cook for 6-7 minutes per side until golden brown and cooked through. Remove and set aside.
  3. In the same skillet, add minced garlic and cook for 30 seconds until fragrant.
  4. Pour in chicken broth and heavy cream, stirring to combine. Add sun-dried tomatoes and bring to a gentle simmer.
  5. Stir in Parmesan cheese until melted and the sauce is creamy.
  6. Add fresh spinach and cook until wilted, about 2 minutes.
  7. Return chicken to the skillet and spoon sauce over top. Simmer for 3-4 minutes to let flavors meld.
  8. Serve hot with pasta, rice, or crusty bread to soak up the delicious sauce.

My Notes

This is one of my favorite weeknight dinners! I like to pound the chicken breasts thin for more even cooking. Sometimes I add a splash of white wine for extra flavor. Pairs perfectly with angel hair pasta.

Spicy Korean Fried Chicken

Ultra-crispy double-fried chicken coated in a sticky, sweet, and spicy Korean gochujang glaze. The secret to the incredible crunch is frying twice and using potato starch in the coating.

Ingredients

  • 2 lbs chicken wings or drumettes
  • 1 cup potato starch
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 3 tablespoons gochujang (Korean chili paste)
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 2 tablespoons honey
  • 1 tablespoon rice vinegar
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tablespoon sesame oil
  • Sesame seeds and green onions for garnish
  • Vegetable oil for frying

Steps

  1. Pat chicken pieces completely dry with paper towels. Season with salt and pepper.
  2. Mix potato starch and flour in a large bowl. Toss chicken pieces to coat thoroughly.
  3. Heat oil to 325°F in a deep pot. Fry chicken in batches for 8-10 minutes until cooked through but not browned. Remove and drain.
  4. Increase oil temperature to 375°F. Fry chicken again for 3-4 minutes until deep golden and extra crispy. Drain on wire rack.
  5. While chicken fries the second time, make the sauce: combine gochujang, soy sauce, honey, rice vinegar, garlic, and sesame oil in a large bowl.
  6. Toss hot fried chicken in the sauce until well coated.
  7. Garnish with sesame seeds and sliced green onions. Serve immediately while crispy!

My Notes

The double-frying technique is CRUCIAL for that signature crunch! Don't skip it. I like to adjust the spice level by adding more or less gochujang. Serve with pickled radish and cold beer for the full Korean fried chicken experience.

Avocado Toast with Poached Eggs

Perfectly poached eggs with runny yolks sit atop creamy smashed avocado on crispy sourdough toast. Finished with red pepper flakes, flaky sea salt, and a drizzle of olive oil for the ultimate breakfast.

Ingredients

  • 2 slices sourdough bread
  • 1 large ripe avocado
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 tablespoon white vinegar
  • Juice of 1/2 lemon
  • Extra virgin olive oil
  • Red pepper flakes
  • Flaky sea salt
  • Fresh cracked black pepper
  • Optional: microgreens or fresh herbs

Steps

  1. Toast sourdough bread until golden and crispy.
  2. Bring a pot of water to a gentle simmer and add white vinegar.
  3. Crack each egg into a small bowl. Create a gentle whirlpool in the water and slide egg into the center. Poach for 3-4 minutes for runny yolks.
  4. While eggs poach, mash avocado in a bowl with lemon juice, salt, and pepper until creamy but still slightly chunky.
  5. Spread mashed avocado generously on toasted bread.
  6. Remove poached eggs with a slotted spoon and gently pat dry with paper towel.
  7. Place poached eggs on top of avocado toast.
  8. Drizzle with olive oil, sprinkle with red pepper flakes, flaky salt, and black pepper. Top with microgreens if desired.

My Notes

My go-to weekend breakfast! The key is using perfectly ripe avocados - they should yield slightly to gentle pressure. I sometimes add everything bagel seasoning instead of the red pepper flakes for variety. Fresh eggs are essential for poaching!

Homemade Ramen

Rich, creamy tonkotsu-style broth simmered to perfection, served with tender chashu pork belly, soft-boiled eggs, and fresh ramen noodles. This Japanese comfort food is worth the time and effort for a truly authentic experience.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb pork bones (or chicken bones)
  • 1 lb pork belly
  • 6 cups chicken stock
  • 2 tablespoons miso paste
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1-inch piece ginger, sliced
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon mirin
  • 4 portions fresh ramen noodles
  • 4 soft-boiled eggs
  • 2 cups fresh spinach or bok choy
  • Green onions, sliced
  • Nori seaweed sheets
  • Sesame oil

Steps

  1. Boil pork bones for 5 minutes, then rinse thoroughly to remove impurities.
  2. In a large pot, combine bones, garlic, ginger, and chicken stock. Simmer for 45 minutes, skimming foam occasionally.
  3. Meanwhile, prepare chashu: roll pork belly tightly, tie with string, and braise in soy sauce, mirin, and water for 30 minutes until tender. Slice when cool.
  4. For soft-boiled eggs: boil for exactly 6.5 minutes, then transfer to ice bath. Peel and marinate in soy sauce mixture.
  5. Strain broth and return to pot. Whisk in miso paste until dissolved. Taste and adjust seasoning with soy sauce.
  6. Cook ramen noodles according to package directions. Blanch spinach or bok choy briefly.
  7. Divide noodles among bowls. Ladle hot broth over noodles.
  8. Top with chashu slices, halved soft-boiled egg, greens, green onions, nori, and a drizzle of sesame oil.

My Notes

This is my special weekend project! The broth can be made ahead and frozen. I love experimenting with different toppings like corn, bamboo shoots, or wood ear mushrooms. The key is the layering of flavors - don't rush the broth!
